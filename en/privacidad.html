Oolia Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 11, 2026

This translation is provided for convenience. If it differs from the Spanish version, the Spanish version prevails.

Oolia is an Android app for tasks, notes, habits and calendar. It is developed by snchi (“we”). This page explains what data the app uses, where it is stored and what you can do with it.

In short: we have no servers. What you write in Oolia is stored on your phone. If you connect your Google account, the app talks directly to Google on your behalf. We never receive, see or store your data. There are no ads, no analytics and no cookies.

What is stored on your phone

Your tasks, notes, folders, tags, habits, journal, focus sessions, attachments (photos, audio, drawings and scans) and the app settings are stored in the app’s private storage on your phone. No other app can read them, and they are deleted if you uninstall Oolia.

In addition, once a day Oolia saves a copy of all of that in the same private area. If you have Android backup (Google One) turned on and your phone has a screen lock, Android uploads it to your Google account with end-to-end encryption: neither Google nor we can read it. It lets the app offer to restore your data if you reinstall Oolia or switch phones. Android manages it, not us: you can turn it off or delete it in Android settings (System, Backup). If the copy doesn’t fit within Android’s limit, it is saved without photos and attachments.

The lock on notes and folders uses your phone’s fingerprint or PIN through the system. The app never sees or stores your fingerprint or PIN.

Your Google account (optional)

You can use Oolia without an account. If you connect your Google account, the app asks for these permissions and uses them only for what is described:

  • Name, email and profile photo: to show you which account you are connected with.
  • Google Calendar: to view, create, change and delete your events and calendars from the app.
  • Google Tasks: to sync your tasks and lists both ways.
  • The app’s private folder in Google Drive (drive.appdata): to store your backups and sync your notes between your devices. It is a hidden folder that only Oolia can see; the app cannot see the rest of your Drive files.

This data travels between your phone and Google, encrypted, and never passes through us. Google handles it under its own privacy policy.

Oolia’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We do not use that data for advertising, we do not sell it, we do not transfer it to anyone and we do not use it to train artificial intelligence models.

Health Connect (optional)

If you give permission, Oolia reads your steps, exercise and sleep from Health Connect to check off the habits that depend on them automatically (for example, “Walk 8,000 steps”). The app only reads that data, never writes anything to Health Connect, and uses it on your phone without sending it anywhere. You can remove the permission at any time in Health Connect settings.

Microphone (optional)

The microphone is used to record audio notes and for dictation. Recordings are saved as attachments on your phone. Dictation is done by your phone’s own speech recognition (the system’s), which may send the audio to its provider depending on your keyboard or phone settings.

Camera and scanner (optional)

Photos and scanned documents you add to a note are stored on your phone. The document scanner is the one from Google Play services and processes images on the phone itself.

Links you paste into a note

When you paste a link and choose to show it as a mention, bookmark or video, the app downloads that website’s title, description and image to show you a preview. For YouTube, TikTok and Vimeo it asks their public preview service. That website receives a normal request from your phone, as if you opened it in the browser.

If you play a video inside the app, that service’s player is loaded, and it may use its own cookies under its policy.

Location, places and maps

When you add a place to an event or a task, you can search for it by name or pick it on a map. For that, the app talks to these public OpenStreetMap services, directly from your phone:

  • Search as you type: what you type is sent to Photon (by komoot). When you tap Search, also to Nominatim (by the OpenStreetMap Foundation).
  • Your approximate location (optional): if you grant that permission, the app uses it to show you what is nearby first. It is sent to Photon along with what you type, as a reference point, and used on your phone to calculate distances. It is the approximate location (neighborhood or city level), never the precise one, and the app neither stores it nor requests it in the background. You can deny or remove it at any time in Android settings; search keeps working without it.
  • The map: map images are downloaded from OpenFreeMap, which receives which area you are viewing, like any map website.
  • A point on the map: when you leave the pin still, its coordinates are sent to Nominatim (or to Photon if it doesn’t answer) to tell you what address is there. You can correct it by hand.

These services receive the request the way any website you visit does (with your connection’s IP address) and handle it under their own policies. The places you choose are stored on your phone, in the recent list.

The mini-game

The mini-game’s high score, shells and Oli’s accessories are stored only on your phone (and in your backups). They are not sent anywhere.

In-app purchases

Premium and tips are paid through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never receive your payment details. The app only checks with Google Play, on your phone, whether the purchase has been made.

What we don’t do

  • We have no servers and no database with your data.
  • We don’t use ads, analytics, tracking or cookies.
  • We don’t sell or share data with anyone.
  • We don’t request your precise location or your location in the background (only the approximate one, if you grant it, for what is explained above).

How long data is kept and how to delete it

  • On your phone: until you delete it or uninstall the app.
  • In Android backup: the latest daily copy, until Android replaces it or you delete it from its settings.
  • In your Google account: events and tasks remain in Google Calendar and Google Tasks, where you can delete them. To delete Oolia’s private folder in Drive (backups and synced notes): on drive.google.com, Settings, Manage apps, Oolia, “Delete hidden app data”.
  • Remove access to your account: at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or from the app’s Settings by disconnecting the account.

Since we don’t store any of your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side. If you have questions, write to us.

Your rights

Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), you have the right to access, correct and delete your data, restrict its use, take it elsewhere and object to its processing. Since all your data is on your phone and in your Google account, you can exercise almost all of those rights yourself: the app lets you export a full copy (Settings, Data) and delete whatever you want. You can also file a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (aepd.es).

Children

Oolia is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from children.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the date above. If the change affects what data the app uses, we will let you know inside the app.
